# Articles of Association
## 1. Application and interpretation
These proposed articles set out how Imarah Technologies (Private) Limited, trading as Imarah.tech, will be managed. They accompany the memorandum and business plan prepared for Assignment 1. They are academic governance proposals, not a claim that the company has been incorporated or these articles have been signed.

In these articles, the company means the proposed startup, members means its shareholders, and the board means its directors acting together. Applicable law takes priority over these articles. Statutory notices, voting procedures, director elections and filing requirements must be followed even where the articles describe a simpler internal working arrangement.

## 2. Private company status
The company will restrict transfers of its shares through the process described below and will not invite the public to subscribe for its shares or other securities. Membership will remain within the statutory limit for a private company. These provisions are intended to preserve its proposed private company status.

## 3. Share capital and ownership
The authorised capital is proposed at PKR 60,000, divided into 600 ordinary shares of PKR 100 each. The initial subscription is proposed at 450 shares totalling PKR 45,000: 150 each for Hadi Abbas, Ayaan Moeen and Saad Hussain. Each founder will therefore hold one-third of the issued ordinary shares and contribute PKR 15,000 over one year.

All initial shares will have equal rights. Each ordinary share carries one vote on a poll and an equal entitlement per share to any lawful dividend or distribution. Subscription amounts and payment dates will be recorded in the share register. No amount will be described as paid-up until it has actually been received.

The annual contribution is PKR 15,000 per founder, not a monthly payment. For cash-flow planning, each founder contributes PKR 1,250 monthly, or PKR 3,750 monthly across the team. Receipts will be recorded as they are paid. The proposed share subscription is not a claim that all shares are already fully paid; allotment and payment terms will be agreed before incorporation.

## 4. New shares and changes in capital
Before proposing a new issue, the board will circulate the purpose, price, proposed recipient and effect on existing ownership. Existing members will receive any pre-emptive rights required by law. Unissued shares do not belong to the founders collectively and cannot be promised by one founder acting alone.

A new investor, employee share arrangement or change in share rights requires the relevant member approval and legal process. No founder has been assigned additional shares, an automatic veto or a separate share class in this proposal.

## 5. Transfer and transmission of shares
A member wishing to sell shares will notify the board in writing of the number of shares and proposed terms. The board will follow the statutory procedure for offering the shares to existing members before an outside transfer. Applicable deadlines, valuation rules and transfer rights will take priority over informal arrangements.

Transfers must be documented and entered in the share register after the required process. A refusal to register a transfer must have a lawful basis and be communicated with reasons. Shares passing through death or succession will be dealt with using appropriate evidence and the applicable legal procedure.

# Directors and management
## 6. First directors and allocation of work
The three founders are proposed as the first directors. This appointment remains subject to eligibility, consent and incorporation requirements. Their initial working responsibilities are as follows.

| Proposed director | Main responsibility | Accountability |
| Hadi Abbas | Model development and technical coordination | Detector evaluation, model releases and compute use |
| Ayaan Moeen | Application and customer operations | Web application, access controls and onboarding |
| Saad Hussain | Data, reporting and evaluation | Dataset records, report quality and test evidence |

These responsibilities do not give a director sole authority over ownership, borrowing or the company's core intellectual property. All directors remain responsible for acting in the company's interests and overseeing its records and finances.

## 7. Appointment, resignation and removal
Directors will be elected, appointed or removed through the procedures required by law, including the applicable notice, voting and representation rights. A vacancy will be filled using the lawful process. Resignation will be recorded in writing and required records and filings will be updated.

Leaving an operational role does not automatically cancel a person's shares. Any share sale, repurchase or other change in ownership needs its own lawful agreement. A director leaving the team must return company access, records and equipment through a documented handover.

## 8. Powers of the board
The board may approve budgets, customer agreements, suppliers, staff appointments and routine product decisions within the company's business scope. It may delegate day-to-day work in writing while retaining oversight. A proposed chief executive or other statutory office holder will be appointed through the appropriate procedure rather than assumed from a project role.

The board will review spending, available cash, customer issues, data incidents and product readiness each month. It will not authorise public release of features that still rely on fictional demonstration data.

## 9. Financial authority
As a proposed internal control, the director responsible for an activity may approve a budgeted expense up to PKR 25,000. Larger expenses require approval from two directors. An unbudgeted commitment above PKR 100,000 requires a recorded board decision before an agreement is signed. Transactions must not be split to avoid these limits.

Bank payments will use two authorised approvers where the banking arrangement permits. Borrowing and personal guarantees require prior board review and the additional member approvals identified in Article 12. No founder may pledge another founder's personal assets or promise a guarantee on their behalf.

## 10. Conflicts of interest
A director must disclose any personal or family interest in a customer, supplier, hiring decision or other proposed transaction before it is considered. The disclosure and decision will be recorded. The interested director will not approve their own transaction or be counted toward an unconflicted decision where prohibited by law.

If too few unconflicted directors remain, the matter will be referred for the required member approval or independent advice. Related-party work must have written terms and a clear business reason.

# Meetings, decisions and records
## 11. Meetings
The board is proposed to meet monthly, with an agenda normally sent at least seven days beforehand. Two eligible, unconflicted directors will form the proposed board quorum, subject to mandatory rules. Remote participation may be used where legally permitted. The chair will be selected by the board and will not have a casting vote under this proposal.

Shareholder meetings will follow the statutory notice and quorum requirements. For the initial three-member structure, the proposed internal quorum is two members representing more than half of the voting shares, unless the law requires more. The annual general meeting and any extraordinary meeting will be convened within the applicable legal requirements.

Minutes will record attendance, conflicts, decisions, votes and assigned actions. Draft minutes will be circulated promptly and approved at the following meeting. A written resolution may be used only where legally permitted and with the required signatures or voting support.

## 12. Voting and reserved decisions
Routine board decisions require a majority of eligible directors present and voting at a quorate meeting. Routine member decisions use the applicable ordinary-resolution process. Each ordinary share has one vote on a poll; other voting methods will follow applicable law.

As an additional proposed internal safeguard, the following matters require affirmative approval from holders of at least 75% of all issued voting shares, together with any legally required resolution, consent or filing:
- Admission of a new equity investor or a change in share rights.
- Sale or exclusive transfer of the company's core product intellectual property.
- Borrowing outside the approved annual budget or granting security over core assets.
- A material change in the business, a merger, or a proposal to wind up the company.

With the proposed equal ownership, all three founders must support these reserved decisions. This internal safeguard does not replace statutory special-resolution rules or legally protected rights of members and creditors.

## 13. Disagreements and deadlock
If a decision is tied or a reserved matter is not approved, the existing arrangement continues unless an urgent legal obligation requires action. The directors will record the disagreement, exchange written options and hold a further meeting within fourteen days.

If the issue remains unresolved, the founders will seek a mutually agreed independent mediator. Mediation will not prevent a party from using a legal remedy or reporting misconduct. No automatic forced sale or discounted buyout is assumed in these articles.

## 14. Records and access
The company will maintain its member and director registers, meeting minutes, share records, accounts, contracts, invoices and required filings. Members will have access to company information in accordance with their legal rights and reasonable confidentiality controls.

Product records will include dataset permissions, model versions, evaluation results and release decisions. Customer records will have restricted access and a documented retention period. Credentials will be stored separately from source code and ordinary company documents. Backups and restoration checks will be assigned to a named director or employee.

# Profits, product duties and closure
## 15. Accounts, remuneration and dividends
The board will prepare management accounts, statutory returns and any required audit. Founder remuneration and reimbursements require documented approval, with conflicts handled under Article 10. Ownership does not create an automatic salary entitlement.

Dividends require lawfully available profits and the required approvals, and must follow share rights. The board will consider taxes, liabilities and working capital first. Founder contributions and customer advances are not distributable profit.

The business plan assumes no dividend in the first three commercial years. This is a planning policy, not a permanent prohibition.

## 16. Product, data and intellectual property duties
Company-created work will be covered by written employment, founder or contractor terms. Existing university obligations and third-party licences will be reviewed before any transfer or commercial release. Customer photographs and records will not become training data without a separate documented permission and a suitable legal basis.

Inspection reports must distinguish detector results, original model observations and a reviewer's assessment. Staff must not remove known limitations or present a model's statement as an engineer's certification. A failed live model request must remain a visible failure or partial result.

Real attendance or protective-equipment monitoring will require an approved policy covering notice, permitted use, access, retention and correction of errors. An unverified detection will not automatically determine wages or disciplinary action. Customers will receive terms explaining the actual scope of the service.

## 17. Amendments
Proposed amendments will be circulated with their purpose and effect. Changes require the applicable resolutions, permissions and filings. Current and earlier approved versions will be retained with adoption records.

## 18. Winding up
Any winding up will follow the applicable statutory process and creditor priorities. A person authorised through that process will settle liabilities and deal with remaining assets. Any lawful surplus will be distributed according to share rights.

Closure must provide for customer data return or permitted deletion, contract settlement, access removal and legally required records. Founders may not divide assets informally while obligations remain unresolved.
